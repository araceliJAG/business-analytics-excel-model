Strategic Operations & Quantitative Forecasting Model: "Donuts to Go" Business ExpansionA comprehensive, four-stage quantitative decision model built in Microsoft Excel to evaluate market demand, simulate production capacity constraints, and guide strategic capital investment (Current Operations vs. Franchise Expansion vs. Mobile Food Truck Launch) under market risk and uncertainty.📖 What This Project Was AboutThe Business Problem:"Donuts to Go" is an operating business facing strategic growth choices. Leadership needed to determine how to scale: should they keep their current brick-and-mortar setup, invest heavily into an expanded franchise model, or pivot into a flexible mobile food truck?Each operational model carried vastly different cost structures, capacity thresholds, and demand exposures:Making too little product leads to stockouts, angry customers, and lost revenue.Making too much product leads to wasted ingredients and forced salvage discounts.Expanding into food trucks requires operating in completely different environments (office parks vs. weekend event venues) with varying demand drivers.The Sequential 4-Stage Architecture:This project was constructed as a strictly dependent, 4-stage pipeline. A calculation mistake or broken reference in Phase 1 or 2 would automatically corrupt downstream monthly P&Ls in Phase 3 and distort the strategic payoff tables in Phase 4. Building this required rigorous data validation, clean cell referencing, and continuous formula auditing across 8 interconnected worksheets.🔍 The 4-Phase Analytical BreakdownPhase 1: Baseline Demand Auditing & Unit EconomicsData Hygiene & Aggregation: Cleaned multi-year historical transactions (2020–2022) using Excel Pivot Tables and dynamic Pivot Charts to isolate monthly seasonal demand trends.Profit & Cost Modeling: Structured baseline unit economics around bundled customer purchases (coffee + donut), categorizing fixed overhead (insurance, rent, maintenance) versus unit variable expenses (ingredients, coffee beans, cups, napkins).Phase 2: Time-Series Decomposition & DeseasonalizationSeasonal Index (SI) Calculation: Quantified cyclical swings by isolating monthly demand against yearly baselines to establish a 3-year average seasonal factor for every month.Demand Normalization: Stripped seasonality out of raw numbers to reveal the business's true underlying growth trajectory.Linear Trend Modeling: Ran simple linear regression ($Y = mx + b$) on 36 months of deseasonalized demand, generating the baseline slope and intercept to project future demand cycles.Phase 3: Operational Constraints & Inventory FinancialsSimulated a 12-month operating calendar for Current Operations (capped at 4,000 units/mo) versus Franchise Operations (capped at 5,280 units/mo) across Low, Average, and High demand states:Capacity Logic: Built dynamic formulas to split demand into satisfied orders versus lost business:$$\text{Satisfied Demand} = \min(\text{Monthly Demand}, \text{Monthly Capacity})$$Overproduction & Salvage Recovery: Evaluated excess inventory and modeled recovery through day-old discounted sales ($1.25/unit).Underproduction & Stockout Penalties: Factored in financial penalties ($3.00/unit) for unsatisfied customer demand to capture lost goodwill and brand damage.Dynamic P&Ls: Structured complete annual financial statements measuring gross revenues, ingredient costs, day-old salvage, stockout penalties, and net annual profit.Phase 4: Multiple Regression & Decision Theory Under UncertaintyPredictive Multiple Regression: Modeled mobile Food Truck demand using historical operating hours and location dummy variables (Event Location vs. Office Location) to project flexible make-to-order demand without surplus waste.Payoff Matrix
